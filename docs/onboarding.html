Onboarding · dm-engine

Get your Instagram replying by itself

Start to finish, on your own account. Every stage ends with a test, so you always know whether to keep going or stop and fix something. No prior Meta developer experience assumed.

~35 min total 9 stages No App Review No company documents $0

What you're building

Someone comments GUIDE on your post. They instantly get a DM with your link and a question: want it by email too? They reply with their address. You have a lead. You were asleep.

That's the whole thing. It's the feature people actually pay ManyChat for.

One decision, already made for you

There are two ways to find out that someone commented. This matters because one of them may be blocked for you and the other can't be.

Polling We start here

Your machine asks Instagram “any new comments?” once a minute.

Delay
Up to 60 seconds
Needs
Nothing beyond a token
Risk
None — can't be gated

Webhooks

Instagram pushes the comment to you the instant it happens.

Delay
Instant
Needs
Public HTTPS + app set to Live
Risk
Meta documents comments as needing Advanced Access

Why not webhooks first

Meta's webhooks page says Advanced Access is required to receive comments notifications — and Advanced Access needs Business Verification, which wants company registration documents. Their App Review page separately says an app serving only an account you own needs no review at all. Those two statements are in tension, and people report it working both ways.

Polling sidesteps the argument entirely. A private reply is allowed up to 7 days after a comment, so checking once a minute loses you nothing. Stage 9 adds webhooks as an upgrade if they turn out to work for you.

1

Prepare the Instagram account

5 min · phone

All in the Instagram mobile app. Three settings, and one of them silently breaks everything if you skip it.

  1. Open Instagram, tap your profile picture (bottom right), then the three lines ☰ at the top right. A panel titled Settings and activity.
  2. Scroll to For professionals → Account type and tools → Switch to professional account. Pick any category, then choose Business or Creator — either works. If it says Switch to personal account instead, you're already professional. Move on.
  3. When it offers to connect a Facebook Page, tap Skip. You genuinely don't need one.
  4. Make the account public: Settings → Account privacy → make sure Private account is off.
  5. Turn on message access: ☰ → Messages and story replies → Message requests → scroll to Connected tools → switch Allow access to messages on. If you can't find Connected tools, carry on — it isn't confirmed as required on this path, but it's free and it's the most common cause of silent failures.

Checkpoint 1

Open a private browser window and visit instagram.com/your-username. You should see your posts without logging in.

If you can't see them: the account is still private. Meta will not report comments on private accounts, and nothing downstream will ever fire. Fix this before continuing.

2

Become a Meta developer

5 min · desktop

Free, instant, and not a commitment to anything. You just need an account that can create apps.

  1. Log into facebook.com in a desktop browser.
  2. Turn on two-factor auth on that Facebook account: Settings & privacy → Settings → Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication. Do it now. Meta blocks several later operations without it, and retrofitting mid-setup is worse.
  3. Go to developers.facebook.com and click Get Started (top right).
  4. Accept the Platform Terms and Developer Policies, then verify your phone by SMS and confirm the emailed code. No credit card is ever requested. If something asks for payment, you're on the wrong page.

Checkpoint 2

Load developers.facebook.com/apps. You should land on an app list (probably empty) with a Create app button — not a login wall.

3

Create the app

5 min

Meta reorganised this screen. Older guides tell you to pick an app type called “Business” — that screen no longer exists. You pick a use case instead.

  1. Click Create app.
  2. App details: enter any App name (it's only visible to you) and your App contact email. Click Next.
  3. Use cases: select the Instagram messaging use case. Click Next. A list of use cases with incompatible ones greyed out. Not a list of app types.
  4. Business: choose I don't want to connect a business portfolio yet. It's optional and you don't need one.
  5. Work through Requirements, then finish on Overview.

Checkpoint 3

Your app appears in the dashboard with a left sidebar. You can see Instagram in that sidebar.

No Instagram in the sidebar: the use case didn't attach. Look for Add product or Products in the sidebar and add Instagram manually.

4

Set permissions — before the token

3 min

Order matters here. A token only carries the permissions that were set when it was generated. Get this backwards and you'll generate a token, wire everything up, and discover at the last step that it can't do anything.

  1. Left sidebar → Instagram → API setup with Instagram business login.
  2. Find Business login settings and confirm these three permissions are present: 
    instagram_business_basic
instagram_business_manage_comments
instagram_business_manage_messages
  3. Save if you changed anything.

Do not skip ahead

If you already generated a token before setting these, generate a fresh one after saving. The old one keeps the old, narrower permissions.

5

Generate your token

3 min
  1. Still in API setup with Instagram business login, section 1. Generate access tokens, click Add account. An Instagram login popup. Log in with the Instagram account from Stage 1 — not your Facebook credentials.
  2. Click Generate token on that account's row. Copy it immediately — it is shown once.
  3. Put it in your terminal: 
    export IG_TOKEN='IGAA...paste-yours...'

Checkpoint 5

Ask Instagram who you are:

curl -s "https://graph.instagram.com/v25.0/me?fields=user_id,username&access_token=$IG_TOKEN"

Expect your own username back, with a user_id.

An OAuthException: the token was copied wrong or has already expired. Generate a new one.
Note: the response may contain both id and user_id. The one that matters is user_id. The engine reads the right one for you.

60 days

Dashboard tokens are long-lived but expire after 60 days, and when they die your automation just stops quietly. Stage 9 covers keeping it alive.

6

Install the engine

5 min · terminal
  1. Set it up (the virtual environment already exists — this just confirms it): 
    cd ~/Automation/dm-engine
python3 -m venv .venv
./.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
  2. Create your settings file: 
    cp .env.example .env
  3. Open .env and set two values. Leave META_DRY_RUN=1 for now — that's the safety catch. 
    # Instagram > API setup > Instagram app secret
META_APP_SECRET=paste-the-instagram-app-secret
# any string you invent; only needed for webhooks later
META_VERIFY_TOKEN=pick-anything-you-like
META_DRY_RUN=1
  4. Connect your account. The settings file is read automatically — nothing to export: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py connect "$IG_TOKEN"

Checkpoint 6

./.venv/bin/python cli.py doctor

Every line should read ok, including Meta accepts the token with your username.

Any FAIL: the line tells you the fix underneath it. Don't continue past a red line — nothing downstream will work.

7

Prove the machinery works

2 min

Before Instagram is involved at all, confirm the engine itself is sound. This talks to nothing external.

  1. In a second terminal window, start the server: 
    cd ~/Automation/dm-engine
./.venv/bin/uvicorn app:app --port 8000
    A line ending Uvicorn running on http://127.0.0.1:8000. Leave it running.
  2. Back in the first window: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py selftest

Checkpoint 7

Eight PASS lines, ending with everything local works. This has proved the server runs, signature checking rejects forgeries, keyword matching fires, the link and email ask ship as one message, and a replied email is captured as a lead.

a correctly signed webhook is accepted fails: the server started before you edited .env. Stop it (Ctrl-C) and start it again.

8

Go live on your real account

10 min

Now the real thing. Still with the safety catch on for the first pass.

  1. Create your automation. Use a keyword you'd genuinely post: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py keyword GUIDE \
  --reply "Here you go: yoursite.com/guide" \
  --ask-email "Want it by email too? Drop your address." \
  --thanks "Sent. Check your inbox."
    A byte count. Meta caps a message at 1000 bytes and you only get one reply per comment, so the link and the ask travel together.
  2. Post something on Instagram. Anything — it just needs to be public and recent.
  3. From your second account (or ask a friend), comment GUIDE on it.
  4. Dry run first — this reads your real comments but sends nothing: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py poll --once
./.venv/bin/python cli.py log
    A logged private_reply with your text. Nothing was actually sent.
  5. If that looked right, take the safety catch off. Edit .env, set META_DRY_RUN=0, then: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py poll --once

Checkpoint 8 — the one that matters

Check the second account's Instagram DMs. Look in the Requests folder too — a first message from an account you don't follow often lands there.

Then finish the loop: reply to that DM with an email address, run cli.py poll --once once more, and:

./.venv/bin/python cli.py leads

Nothing in the log at all: the comment is older than 7 days, or the keyword didn't match — matching is on whole words, so GUIDE won't match GUIDES.
A FAIL line mentioning permissions: copy the full error. That's the Advanced Access question landing on you, and it's the one thing that would mean filing for App Review.
Reply sent but no lead captured: the reply has to come back as a DM within 24 hours. Check cli.py log for outside 24h messaging window.

9

Keep it running

3 min
  1. Install it as a background service. It starts when you log in, restarts if it dies, and checks every 60 seconds: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py install
    A confirmation with the log file path. cli.py doctor now shows whether it's running. cli.py uninstall stops it.
  2. If you edit .env later, the service needs a restart: cli.py uninstall then cli.py install.
  3. Put a monthly reminder in your calendar to run: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py refresh
    Tokens die at 60 days and the failure is silent — replies just stop.
Day to dayWhat it tells you
cli.py leadsEvery email you've captured
cli.py logWhat went out, and what failed and why
cli.py statsHeadroom against Meta's 750/hour cap
cli.py doctorToken health and config
+

Optional: make it smarter

5 min each · all off by default

Everything above sends a fixed message. These are the upgrades, and each is one flag.

  1. AI replies — answers what people actually asked instead of dumping a link. Put ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=... in .env, then add a brief to a keyword: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py keyword GUIDE \
  --reply "Here you go: yoursite.com/guide" \
  --ai-brief "We sell handmade candles from Bristol. UK shipping is 3 days, GBP 4.50."
    The fixed --reply becomes the fallback — used whenever the model is unavailable or declines. A comment can't talk it into inventing prices or promising refunds; the commenter's words are treated as data, not instructions.
  2. Follow-ups — one nudge to people who replied but never handed over an email: 
    ./.venv/bin/python cli.py keyword GUIDE --reply "..." --ask-email "..." \
  --followup "Still want that guide? Just send your email." --followup-after 4
    Meta only allows a DM within 24 hours of their last message, so someone who never replied at all can't be nudged. Those show as expired in cli.py log with the reason.
  3. Leads into your email tool — set LEAD_WEBHOOK_URL in .env to a Zapier, Make or n8n catch-hook. Every lead is POSTed there as it lands, with retries. It's saved locally first, so a broken endpoint never loses one.
+

Optional: instant replies via webhooks

20 min · only if you want it

Polling costs you up to a minute. If that bothers you, try webhooks — but only after Stage 8 works. If this stage fails, nothing is lost; you stay on polling.

  1. Set the app to Live in the dashboard. Look for a mode control near the app name. Going Live is a toggle, not App Review — people conflate these constantly. Webhooks never fire in Development mode.
  2. Get a public HTTPS address for your machine: 
    brew install cloudflared
cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:8000
    A https://something.trycloudflare.com URL. It changes every restart.
  3. In Instagram → Webhooks, set the Callback URL to https://your-tunnel-url/webhook and the Verify token to your META_VERIFY_TOKEN. Save. Then subscribe to the comments and messages fields.
  4. Subscribe the account itself. This step is easy to miss and everything silently fails without it: 
    curl -X POST "https://graph.instagram.com/v25.0/me/subscribed_apps" \
  -d "subscribed_fields=comments,messages" \
  -d "access_token=$IG_TOKEN"
  5. Comment on a post from your second account and watch the uvicorn window.

Reading the result

A comment event arrives: you have instant replies. Stop the poller.

messages arrives but comments never does: that's the documented Advanced Access gate, real and enforced for you. Keep the poller running — same outcome, 60 seconds slower.

Nothing arrives at all: plumbing, not policy. Check the app is Live, that step 4 actually applied, and that your tunnel URL is current.
